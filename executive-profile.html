What the work was for, and what changed

Two operating contexts — faculty affairs at institutional scale, and high-volume advancement operations. Every figure below is drawn from the professional record.

Harvard · FAS Office for Faculty Affairs

Academic and faculty affairs operations

2019 – 2025
50+Department chairs advised, with materials for FAS deans, visiting committees, and the Office of the Governing Boards.
67–75%Fewer one-off requests. At least 67% if the earlier year was 300, and 75% if it was 400. The later volume was under 100.

I piloted FAS DataHub (Oracle BI / HART) — a team effort that used my faculty-affairs reports as its blueprint. It joined seven sources inside a $1.8 billion operating environment with more than twenty years of longitudinal data, and served FAS Finance, the Office of the Governing Boards, the Graduate School and Harvard College as well as Faculty Affairs.

Reusable dashboards replaced the ad-hoc request queue, so the same questions stopped arriving as bespoke asks.

The discipline was layered records: a constituent profile first, giving records on that constituent, and campaign finance counted from the giving records — not from a parallel spreadsheet.

Later, at Phillips Academy Andover, the same method supported a $400 million campaign and General Ledger bridge reconciliations with the Comptroller’s office.

SMARTER · a decision-and-delivery operating cycle

12 months · First-year operating releases Prioritize, baseline, pilot, and deploy selected workstreams. Gate 1 establishes evidence; first-year completion is not all benefits realized.
18 months · Proposed delivery governance Complete longer-running integrations, handoffs, and formal release checks through the proposed G0–G5 governance arc.
36 months · Economic and benefits horizon Phase costs, verify sustained adoption, and evaluate outcomes; sponsor and academic due dates are separate hard constraints.
The guardrail DBMI coordinates planning, evidence, and follow-through. Approval authority stays with the offices that hold it — described in the table below.
